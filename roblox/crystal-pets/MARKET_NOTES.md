# Market notes (researched 2026-11-28)

Directional only. Several sources are marketing content and disagree with each other.

## Genres
- Sources conflict on the top earner. Some say simulators (pet collecting, progression) are still the most profitable, but also the most crowded. Others say RPGs, and one 2026 ranking says the biggest earners lean on cosmetic spending (Rivals, Dress to Impress, Brookhaven).
- Tycoons earn mostly through passes for faster progression, exclusive zones and aesthetics.
- Takeaway: Crystal Pets (pet sim plus tycoon-style zones) is a reasonable pick. Differentiate with a theme and strong retention, and add a cosmetics layer later.

## Payouts
- Roblox announced (April 2026) a 42% higher DevEx rate for Robux earned from verified US 18+ players, effective June 8, 2026. It applies to passes, subscriptions, select in-game items and private servers. Reported rates: about $0.0038 per Robux standard, about $0.0054 per Robux for 18+.
- Payout rates were also raised 8.5% in September 2025.
- Roblox paired this with discovery changes for "novel" games ("Standout Games" home section). Originality is rewarded, so the theme and hooks matter.
- Takeaway: games that appeal to older players earn more per Robux. Consider adult-friendly content (deeper progression, trading and collection) after launch.

# How top earners earn (researched 2026-11-28, second pass)

Mostly blogs and tracker sites, so the numbers are rough. None are official per-game disclosures.

## Who earns what
- Roblox says that in the 12 months to June 30, 2026 the top 10 creators averaged about $65.7M each, the top 100 about $10.8M, and the top 1,000 about $1.4M. The top 10 took about 39% of the $1.7B payout pool.
- The median DevEx creator earned about $1,500. Most games earn very little, and revenue is extremely concentrated.
- Top-10 figures can cover teams and studios, not single people.

## What the biggest hits do
- Steal a Brainrot (trackers estimate roughly $2-3.5M per month) and Grow a Garden (estimates range from $0.4M to $1.2M per month, with huge lifetime claims). Trackers disagree widely, and neither has published official revenue.
- Both are viral, short-build games that sell consumables, boosts and progression skips repeatedly.
- Lifetime revenue figures derived from visits only ever go up, even when the live audience collapses, so treat them as upper bounds.

## Monetization patterns (blog consensus, not Roblox data)
- Developer products (repeatable) drive most revenue in high earners. Game passes are one-time perks. Many guides recommend one anchor method per game.
- Subscriptions are described as the steadiest recurring revenue. This game has none yet.
- Creator Rewards reportedly replaced Premium Payouts in July 2025. Sources conflict, so check the Creator Hub.
- Real benchmarks for conversion rate and revenue per paying user were not found. The only ARPPU figure (100-300 Robux) comes from one tycoon case study.
- Revenue share: sources say the developer keeps about 70% on passes, products and subscriptions, then DevEx converts Robux to USD at about $0.0038 per Robux (about $0.0054 for verified US 18+ spend), so real-money take-home is roughly a quarter to a third of what players spend.

## What this means for Crystal Pets
1. Lean on repeatable developer products (coin packs, boosts, cosmetic bundles). They are already in the shop. Add a prompt at moments of need: for example offer the 2x Boost right after a rebirth or when a player can't afford an egg.
2. Subscription ("Crystal Club": x1.5 coins, exclusive trail, bonus pet slot). Built. Needs a subscription ID.
3. Treat a launch as a lottery. The distribution is brutal, so plan on fast iteration, outside promotion (creators, social) and weekly updates, not one big release.
4. Keep content original and themed. Roblox is promoting novel games, and the market is full of clones.
